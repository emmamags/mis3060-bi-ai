# Business Context Exploration
## Question 1:
### 1. Exact Prompt  
"The portfolio provides 3 borrower attributes (credit score, debt-to-income ratio, annual income). What makes a credit score an important and unique attribute to include in comparison to the other three? How highly should a portfolio manager regard the stat?"
### 2. Summary
Claude states that credit score is very important to include as a borrowing attribute because it tracks a borrower's repayment history over time. DTI and annual income, on the other hand, reflect only current financial ability and don't account for the borrower's habits and past behavior. It also states that "credit score is the clearest driver of risk differentiation," which means that, for a portfolio manager, credit score should rank highest among the three stats. 
### 3. Follow-up Question
How does credit score incorporate qualitative variables like defaults on payments into a quantitative score? 
## Question 2:
### 1. Exact Prompt
"Given Wildcat’s mix of Auto, Personal, Home Improvement, Education, and Business loans, what strategic issues should a portfolio committee prioritize in a quarterly review, particularly for a lender with significant exposure to Education and Home Improvement loans?"
### 2. Summary
Although home improvement is the most common of the four loan types, it consistently underperforms, and portfolio managers should account for this when reviewing and underwriting loans, and consider scaling back approvals and increasing rates. Education loans carry less risk, but their performance has declined in recent years, so this sector also needs monitoring. Overall, identifying problems in the lending market early will help the lender make more informed growth and pricing decisions.
### 3. Follow-up Question
How do you know/where did you find that information that education loans are now carrying more risk than they did in the past? And why is that happening?
## Question 3:
### 1. Exact Prompt
"The portfolio has four status categories (Current, Paid Off, Default, Delinquent). Explain the difference between delinquency and default and how that maps onto Wildcat's four status categories"
### 2. Summary
Delinquency means a borrower has missed payments on a loan, but the loan is still active and can either recover or eventually default. On the other hand, defaulting on a loan means the borrower is no longer expected to repay the agreed amount. Claude states that delinquency should be regarded as an early sign of credit risk and taken into account when underwriting loans; however, the wildcat portfolio does not show whether delinquent loans eventually default or become active again, so we cannot see the true outcome.  
### 3. Follow-up Question
How heavily does delinquency affect a borrower's credit score? How about defaulting on a loan?
## Question 4:
### 1. Exact Prompt
"Given the growth or depreciation of Auto, Personal, Home Improvement, Education, and Business loans, how should the Wildcat portfolio seek to balance these changes and maintain a prospering portfolio?"
### 2. Summary
Claude suggested that the loan mix should be actively monitored and should not simply follow natural market growth. For example, home improvement loans might be growing, but they are also very risky, so it's important to include some of the less prominent but low-risk loans to diversify the portfolio. Overall, the portfolio should intentionally include a mix of all loan types.
### 3. Follow-up Question
If one loan type were to be removed, which one would have the least impact on the portfolio?
## Question 5:
### 1. Exact Prompt
"Should the portfolio managers use the same underwriting standards for all loan types (Auto, Personal, Home Improvement, Education, and Business)? Or should each segment have different requirements? Explain why?"
### 2. Summary
Claude stated that each segment should be held to different standards because they have "different repayment risks, collateral, and loan sizes." For example, a business loan is going to be much bigger than an average car loan, so the standard for the person taking out the business loan is going to be much higher than that of the person with the car loan. The bank needs to consider not only the borrower's reliability but also how much they can afford to lose. 
### 3. Follow-up Question
Are bigger banks or smaller banks more willing to take on riskier loans?
